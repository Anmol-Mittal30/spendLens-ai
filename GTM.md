# GTM

The exact target user is a technical founder, CTO, or head of engineering at a 5-50 person seed to Series A startup that adopted AI tools quickly and now has a messy stack: Cursor for engineers, ChatGPT Team for ops, Claude Max for founders, Copilot seats from last year, and API usage nobody has benchmarked recently. They are not looking for a procurement suite. They want a quick second opinion before renewing or expanding AI spend.

The trigger moment is usually frustration: a founder sees a larger-than-expected OpenAI or Anthropic invoice, an engineering manager notices inactive paid seats, or a finance lead asks why the AI line item doubled. They might Google "Cursor Business worth it", "ChatGPT Team vs Pro", "Claude Max cost", "OpenAI API bill too high", or scroll X posts about startups wasting money on AI subscriptions.

The first distribution channels should be specific and manual: Indie Hackers, r/SaaS, r/startups, Hacker News "Show HN", founder Slack groups, Cursor/Claude power-user threads on X, and college entrepreneurship WhatsApp/Discord groups where early founders ask practical tooling questions. I would also reply to public posts where founders complain about AI bills with a short personalized teardown and the tool link.

The first 100 users in 30 days should come from direct usefulness, not paid ads. I would do 25 manual audits for founders in my network and ask permission to anonymize the results. Then I would publish a short teardown thread: "I audited 25 startup AI stacks; 14 were overspending by more than $300/mo." Each example would link to SpendLens. I would submit a Show HN with the angle "Mint for AI tool spend" and post in communities with the actual anonymized findings.

Credex has the unfair channel: it can turn audit results into real savings through discounted AI credits, not just advice. A generic calculator can say "spend less"; Credex can say "you are paying retail and we can help you capture part of this gap."

Week-1 traction that suggests this works: 150 completed audits, 35 email captures, 10 high-savings consult requests, 3 booked calls, and at least one founder sharing their public audit URL without being asked.
